    Clones (virtual asteroids)¶

    An orbit is known only within its errors. A clone, or virtual asteroid, is one orbit the observations allow; the nominal orbit is one of them, "without any special significance" (Milani, Chesley & Valsecchi 2000, p. 947). A run with N clones of an asteroid classifies each clone like any body and reports, per resonance, the share of clones with each status and its 95 % interval: the probability of a status given the provider's orbit solution, the package's model and the integration span. It says nothing about the model's or the classifier's own errors.

    import resonances


def main():
    solution = resonances.orbit_solution(463, source='astdys')  # nominal orbit + covariance (cached in cache/clones/)
    ensemble = solution.sample(n=10, seed=0)  # 10 clones and the nominal; 100-1000 for real shares

    sim = resonances.Simulation(name='clones-demo', date='2026-01-01', integration_years=2_000)
    sim.create_solar_system()
    sim.add_ensemble(ensemble, resonances=['4J-2S-1'])  # clones drawn at another epoch are integrated to the date first
    sim.run()
    shares = sim.clone_summary  # the rows of clones.csv: the share of each status with its 95 % Wilson interval
    print(shares[['resonance', 'n_clones', 'nominal_status', 'modal_status', 'modal_share', 'f_resonant_lo', 'f_resonant_hi']])


if __name__ == '__main__':  # the clones run in worker processes: the guard is required
    main()

    The clones run in worker processes, so a script needs the if __name__ == '__main__': guard (without it a run on macOS or Windows stops with an error that names the missing guard). Use 100–1000 clones and the full span for real shares.

    Short forms: sim.add_body(463, '4J-2S-1', clones=100), resonances.check(463, '4J-2S-1', clones=100), resonances.find(463, clones=100) (candidates from the whole cloud), CLONES=100 in .env, and on the command line resonances check --asteroids=463 --resonances=4J-2S-1 --clones=100. The body itself is then added from DATA_SOURCE as without clones; its clones come from the orbit solution of CLONES_SOURCE (auto = the same provider: AstDyS for astdys, MPC for mpc, JPL SBDB for nasa).

    Your own clones: resonances.Ensemble.from_elements('name', rows, epoch_mjd=..., nominal=...) (rows with a, e, inc, Omega, omega, M in radians, one epoch), or an ensemble saved by Ensemble.save / read by Ensemble.load.

    Where the covariance comes from¶

    An orbit is fitted to the astrometry by least squares. With the residuals linearized around the best fit X₀, the orbit's density is approximated by the Gaussian

    \[ p(X) \propto \exp\!\left(-\tfrac12\,\Delta X^{\mathsf T} C^{-1} \Delta X\right), \qquad \Delta X = X - X_0, \qquad C = (B^{\mathsf T} W B)^{-1}, \]

    B the derivatives of the observations by the six coordinates, W their weights. The clones use the Gaussian approximation in the published covariance coordinates: C as each provider publishes it, in its coordinates and at one epoch (whether these are the coordinates the fit was solved in is not stated). A Gaussian is a local approximation and depends on the coordinates; carrying its draws through the nonlinear map does not repair a poor one. The shares below are probabilities under this approximation, not the true astrometric posterior:

    source coordinates X units epoch file
    astdys (numbered) equinoctial a, h = e sin ϖ, k = e cos ϖ, p = tan(i/2) sin Ω, q = tan(i/2) cos Ω, λ au, λ in degrees (in the covariance too) .eq1: the catalog's standard epoch (default); .eq0: the fit's own OEF2.0 newton.spacedys.com/~astdys2/epoch/numbered/<n/1000>/<n>.eq1
    neodys (NEAs) as AstDyS as AstDyS .eq0: the fit's own, near the middle of the arc (default); .eq1 ~neodys2/epoch/<designation>.eq0
    sbdb (JPL) cometary e, q, t_p, Ω, ω, i au, JD TDB, degrees the orbit-solution epoch (not the epoch of the listed elements) ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/sbdb.api?sstr=..&cov=mat&full-prec=1
    mpc Cartesian x, y, z, vx, vy, vz (heliocentric) au, au/day the orbit's epoch data.minorplanetcenter.net/api/get-orb

    All in the J2000 ecliptic, heliocentric, as the package; a file stating another frame (OEF refsys other than ECLM J2000, MPC system_data not ecliptic/ICRF or another obliquity than 84381.448″, SBDB equinox not J2000) is refused, nothing is rotated. An OEF file is read as one record: a second one (a bulk .ctc) is refused. The units were checked on the providers' own numbers (tests in tests/resonances/clones/): the OEF EIG line is √eig(C) only with λ converted to radians; MPC's eigenvalues are √eig of its CAR covariance; MPC's CAR and COM blocks give its own KEP block; AstDyS and MPC agree on 463 to 3·10⁻⁸ au.

    A file is downloaded once into CLONES_CACHE_DIR/<source>/ with <file>.meta.json (URL, download time, sha256); orbit_solution(..., refresh=True) fetches it again (an orbit improves with new observations). The ensemble's JSON keeps the whole solution.

    Why a multivariate normal in the published coordinates¶

    The coordinates are strongly correlated: in JPL's covariance of 463, |corr(e, q)| = 0.99999. Independent errors per element, as several papers draw them, put the clones outside the orbit's confidence region; with e and q independent σ_a comes out 4–900 times too large. Each clone is therefore

    \[ X_i = X_0 + L z_i, \qquad C = L L^{\mathsf T}\ \text{(Cholesky)}, \qquad z_i \sim \mathcal N(0, I_6), \]

    in the published coordinates, and converted exactly to Keplerian elements (equinoctial: e = √(h² + k²), ϖ = atan2(h, k), i = 2 atan √(p² + q²), Ω = atan2(p, q), ω = ϖ − Ω, M = λ − ϖ; cometary: a = q/(1 − e), M = k a^−3/2 (t − t_p); Cartesian: the two-body map with μ = k², the phase from the argument of latitude, so a circular orbit keeps it). L is computed from the correlation matrix (a covariance of a good orbit has a condition number of 10¹³–10¹⁹ in its raw units). A draw that is no bound ellipse (e < 0, e ≥ 1 or a ≤ 0) is kept in the ensemble (valid False), never redrawn, and never integrated: it is no observation of any status, so it is left out of the shares. When such draws are more than CLONES_MAX_INVALID (5 %) of the sample, the Gaussian is no usable model of the orbit's uncertainty (a linear covariance of a 1-day arc: SBDB gives 2010 CG236 σ_e = 13, all 100 draws unbound; 2010 DH65 83 of 100) and its clones.csv row gets no shares at all (cloud_usable False).

    Each clone's \(d^2 = \Delta X^{\mathsf T} C^{-1} \Delta X = \lvert z\rvert^2\) is stored: it follows \(\chi^2\) with 6 degrees of freedom. When float64 finds C singular (Cholesky fails), the eigen factor keeps its rank modes (Ensemble.rank, in the JSON as covariance_rank) and d² is the pseudo-inverse distance ΔXᵀ C⁺ ΔX, χ² with rank degrees of freedom. In six dimensions the "3σ" ellipsoid d ≤ 3 holds only 82.6 % of the clones (P(χ²₆ ≤ 9) = 1 − e^−4.5 (1 + 4.5 + 4.5²/2)); 95 % lie within d ≤ 3.55. The draws are not truncated.

    The draws of a body are seeded by (CLONES_SEED, crc32(name)) and prefix-stable: the first 100 of 400 clones are the 100 clones.

    Why the covariance's own epoch¶

    .eq1 is the .eq0 covariance propagated linearly, C₁ = J C₀ Jᵀ. For an NEA observed over a short arc years ago this no longer describes the orbits: for 2013 RZ53 (a 3-day arc in 2013) σ_a grows 47 times and σ_λ 359 times from .eq0 to .eq1, through Earth encounters a linear map does not follow, and the .eq1 matrix is numerically indefinite. The clones are drawn where the fit was made and each one is integrated to the run's date (IAS15, the run's planets, propagate.py; the nominal alone, the clones together); all of them then start together. Hence the defaults .eq0 for NEODyS, the solution epoch for SBDB, and MPC refused for U ≥ 3 (MPC publishes the covariance only at its standard epoch; U ≥ 3 is a conservative policy, not a physical boundary). For numbered main-belt asteroids .eq1 is fine and needs no integration (its epoch is AstDyS's catalog epoch).

    Any gap between the covariance's epoch and the run's date is crossed: up to 10⁻⁴ d (8.6 s) on the two-body ellipses (5 s already move 463's mean anomaly by 3.8 σ_λ), beyond that by integration. clones.csv records the span (propagation_days) and the way (propagation: none, kepler, ias15). The integration was validated against a provider's own propagation only for MPC elements over 138 days (Configuration, MPC_EPOCH_POLICY); with MPC_PROPAGATE_MAX_DAYS set, a longer span of the clones gets a warning (not a refusal). A timezone-aware run date is taken as its UTC value, the same instant for the planets and the clones.

    The carried cloud is in the package's model: no relativity, no asteroid perturbers, no non-gravitational force. A body added from another source (the short forms) can then lie off its cloud: nominal_offset_sigma in clones.csv is max(|Δa|/sd(a), |Δλ|/sd(λ)) of the body against its clones at the date, with a warning above 3.

    Non-gravitational parameters (Yarkovsky A2, comet-like A1–A3) are not modeled: the orbital 6×6 block of the covariance (the marginal over them) is used, a warning is logged and clones.csv says so (nongrav).

    Main belt. A numbered main-belt asteroid has σ_a ~ 10⁻⁹ au, orders of magnitude below any resonance width: its clones differ only through chaos, and a regular orbit gives the nominal's verdict to every clone.

    How many clones¶

    A share k/N is a binomial estimate; its 95 % interval is Wilson's score interval (Wilson 1927), with \(\hat p = k/N\) and \(z = 1.96\):

    \[ \text{center} = \frac{\hat p + z^2/2N}{1 + z^2/N}, \qquad \text{half-width} = \frac{z}{1 + z^2/N}\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1 - \hat p)}{N} + \frac{z^2}{4N^2}}, \]

    which stays within [0, 1] and is not empty at 0/N or N/N.

    N half-width at p = 0.5 0 of N: upper bound use
    100 (default) ±10 % 3.7 % resonant, split or not
    400 ±5 % 0.95 % the share itself is the result
    1000 ±3 % 0.38 % rare outcomes

    Integration is cheap (about 0.04 s per clone per 10 kyr with SABA); the classification is not: 1–2 CPU-min per clone and resonance for a 1-Myr record. Choose the span and the output step with that in mind.

    How a run treats them¶

    • The nominal is an ordinary body of the run: in its own simulation, as without clones, so its verdict does not change.
    • The clones <name>~c001, ... are test particles run after the ordinary bodies by the batch workers, CLONES_BATCH (20) per REBOUND simulation, on BATCH_N_CORES processes. Each has its rows in summary.csv and, by SAVE, its data files; its own figures only with CLONES_PLOT_EACH=True.
    • In a find (resonances.find(..., clones=N) or add_ensemble(ens) without resonances), every clone gets the union of the MMR candidates of the whole cloud (a clone may sit in a resonance the nominal never visits); the nominal keeps its own, so its rows and the OUTPUT_STEP=auto step planned from them are those of a run without clones. The clones run at that step; a clone-only row it undersamples is flagged by the analysis' own sampling caps.

    Output¶

    • clones.csv (top of save_path), one row per body and resonance: n_clones, n_classified, n_missing, n_draw_invalid (draws that are no bound orbit, not integrated), f_draw_invalid (their share of n_clones), cloud_usable (f_draw_invalid ≤ CLONES_MAX_INVALID); for each status n_<status>, f_<status>, f_<status>_lo, f_<status>_hi (Wilson 95 %); modal_status, modal_share; nominal_status, nominal_confidence, nominal_agreement (share of clones with the nominal's status, with _lo, _hi: a diagnostic, the nominal has no special weight); nominal_offset_sigma; source, product (eq0, eq1, sbdb, get-orb), epoch_mjd (the covariance's), run_date_mjd, propagated, propagation (none, kepler, ias15), propagation_days (run date − epoch), seed, nongrav. The shares' denominator is n_classified: the bound clones with a verdict (the unbound draws are not in it, and invalid is only the classifier's verdict on a clone's record; clones without a verdict are n_missing, left out). A row with cloud_usable False keeps its counts but its shares, modal status and nominal agreement are empty. A kinematic Lidov-Kozai libration and one without a gate verdict stay apart (why).
    • data/ensemble-<name>.csv and .json: every draw (clone index and name, Keplerian elements at the covariance's epoch, valid, d2, the draw in the fit's coordinates x_<label>, the elements at the run's date <element>_start) and the orbit solution with its provenance. resonances.Ensemble.load reads it back (to the bit).
    • data/ensemble-<name>-results.npz (with SAVE on, kept): every clone's verdict per row (status, confidence, angle_status, reason, libration_origin), its initial a, half-widths and derived rows, and the angles of the clones and of the nominal thinned to 2000 points for the figure (years float64, degrees float32: a thumbnail, never analyzed). Restore reads it (below).
    • figures/<layout>/<name>-<resonance>-clones.png (plot kind clones, also drawn with combined; in the folder of the nominal's row; drawn when PLOT selects the nominal's row or any clone's): (a) the angle of every clone colored by its status, the nominal in black; (b) the status of every clone against its initial a (a jitter separates the points vertically), with the catalog resonant axis \(a_{\rm res}\) of an MMR (dotted, legend a_res (catalog)) and \(a_{\rm res}\) ± the median libration half-width the clones' episodes measured (dashed); (c) the shares with their Wilson intervals, the nominal's status starred (How to read the figures).

    The settings (CLONES, CLONES_SOURCE, CLONES_EPOCH, CLONES_SEED, CLONES_BATCH, CLONES_PLOT_EACH, CLONES_CACHE_DIR) are listed in Configuration.

    Resume and restore¶

    Resume. A run with clones keeps one checkpoint for the ordinary bodies and the clones, clones_state/ in save_path, and removes it (and simulation_state.json) only after the whole run has succeeded. An interrupted run is resumed by running the same script again with the same save_path (resume_enabled, on by default):

    • each finished clone batch left clones_state/batch-<i>.npz (published atomically; checked when read: run, members, rows, numbers): it is not run again; a batch without a valid file runs again and its old rows are replaced;
    • a finished ordinary part is not run again: in one simulation its verdicts are read back from summary.csv (this needs save_summary; without it the ordinary bodies run again), in batches through simulation_state.json. The nominal is never moved into another grouping (its trajectory depends on its simulation's particles);
    • the run's identity is checked first: the ordinary bodies with their elements and grouping, the planets' initial state, the settings, the clone settings and batches, each ensemble (the provider's file sha256, the solution, the draws, seed and rank) and the clones' starting elements at the date, which clones_state/starts.npz keeps and a resume compares bit for bit; and the versions of resonances, rebound and numpy. Any difference raises ValueError saying what changed (an orbit solution fetched again with orbit_solution(..., refresh=True) is another cloud). To start over, remove clones_state/.

    Restore. simulation.json lists the ensembles (simulation.ensembles, Serialization); SimulationSerializer.restore rebuilds sim.ensembles (names, starting elements, the stored verdicts and thumbnails) and sim.clone_summary (clones.csv), and ensemble_run.plot_ensembles draws the figure again. With SAVE off there is no manifest: only sim.clone_summary comes back (a warning). recompute_librations=True reclassifies the nominal from its saved series; the clones keep their stored verdicts (clone_verdicts = 'stored'), and the nominal columns of clones.csv (nominal_status, nominal_confidence, nominal_agreement) and the nominal's summary.csv rows are rewritten in the run's folder from the one new verdict. The clones are not reclassified even when their series were saved: a SAVE mode other than all keeps the series of the clones its status filter selected, so recomputing only those would bias the shares, and each clone costs as much analysis as the nominal.

    Limits¶

    Not done: a clone's own series are not reclassified by a restore; no line-of-variations sampling or statistical ranging for orbits too weak for a Gaussian; no weighted ensembles.

    No shortcut that gives every clone the nominal's verdict without classifying it. A small spread of the cloud in a does not certify that every clone shares the nominal's verdict: chaos inside a resonance separates clones of any spread over a long enough span (33128 6N-7 librates with a Lyapunov time of about 4 kyr), the classifier has thresholds a tiny difference can cross, and a nominal from another source can lie off its own cloud (nominal_offset_sigma). And integrating the clones is cheap next to classifying them, so skipping the integration would save little.

A. & Cefola, P. J. (1972), Celest. Mech. 5, 303 (equinoctial elements); Wilson, E. B. (1927), JASA 22, 209; Newcombe, R. G. (1998), Stat. Med. 17, 857.
