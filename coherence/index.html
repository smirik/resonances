    Cross-spectral coherence¶

    Periodograms answer "which periods are present in this series". Cross spectra answer a different question: do two series share a period, and with what phase shift?

    That is what distinguishes a real resonance from a coincidence:

    • in a mean-motion resonance, the resonant angle \(\sigma\) and the semi-major axis share the libration frequency, a quarter period apart. From \(\dot\sigma = c\,(a - a_*)\) with \(c = -3kn/(2a)\), a libration \(\sigma = A\sin\omega t\) gives \(a - a_* = (A\omega/c)\cos\omega t\): \(a\) leads \(\sigma\) by +90° when \(c > 0\) (the usual prograde angle, \(k < 0\)) and by −90° when \(c < 0\). The preset target below is +90°, a convention for the usual sign, not a law (The resonant pendulum);
    • in a Lidov-Kozai resonance, \(e\) and \(i\) share the ZLK-cycle frequency in antiphase: the body trades eccentricity for inclination at constant \(H = \sqrt{1-e^2}\cos i\).

    The second point is not decorative. The argument of pericentre ω can librate without any Lidov-Kozai resonance: when the forced eccentricity vector dominates the free one, the geometry alone keeps ω bounded. Asteroid 591986 is exactly this case — its osculating ω librates, but its free ω circulates; at its 6-kyr cycle e and i move in phase (a forced-eccentricity beat), not in the ZLK antiphase.

    No status depends on this page

    The cross spectra are diagnostics. The e-i criterion gives the columns zlk_coherent and zlk_check (confirmed_ei is now the e–i coupling at the row's own period, Free elements); what makes 591986 non_resonant is the free-ω gate (Free elements). The reason is the baseline, see below.

    How it is measured¶

    Welch's method on non-overlapping segments, via scipy.signal.coherence and scipy.signal.csd with identical parameters. Each of the \(n_{\rm seg}\) segments is linearly detrended and windowed (Hann by default), the two series are Fourier transformed (\(X\), \(Y\)), and the spectra are averaged over segments: \(P_{xx} = \langle |X|^2\rangle\), \(P_{yy} = \langle |Y|^2\rangle\), \(P_{xy} = \langle X^* Y\rangle\). Then

    \[ \gamma^2(f) = \frac{|P_{xy}|^2}{P_{xx}P_{yy}} \in [0, 1], \qquad \varphi(f) = \arg P_{xy}, \qquad \text{amplitude ratio} = \sqrt{P_{yy}/P_{xx}} . \]

    \(\gamma^2\) is the magnitude-squared coherence (1: the two series share that frequency with a fixed phase); a positive \(\varphi\) means the second series leads.

    Non-overlapping is deliberate. The significance threshold

    \[ \gamma^2_{\rm crit} = 1 - \alpha^{1/(n_{\rm seg} - 1)} \]

    is exact only for statistically independent segments. Measured false-alarm rate on white noise, alpha = 0.01:

    segmentation gamma2_crit measured fraction of significant bins
    non-overlapping 0.785 (n=4) 0.0100
    50% overlap 0.785 (n=4) 0.0001

    With overlap the same threshold is roughly a hundred times too conservative. \(\alpha\) is a false-alarm probability per frequency bin, not for the whole searched spectrum.

    Validity limits¶

    \[ P_{\min} = 4\,\Delta t \quad\text{(a margin over Nyquist)}, \qquad P_{\max} = \frac{T}{5\,n_{\rm seg}} \quad\text{(at least 5 cycles in every segment)}. \]

    Nothing outside [P_min, P_max] is reported. This is the constraint that usually bites: to see a 50 kyr Lidov-Kozai cycle at n_seg = 4 you need at least 1 Myr of integration. A shorter run reports short_baseline rather than a verdict.

    Segmentation levels¶

    P_max shrinks as n_seg grows, while the threshold gets easier. Rather than fixing one segmentation, several levels are computed (COHERENCE_N_SEGMENTS, default 4,8,16) and the verdict is taken from the coarsest level whose validity band is non-empty — the one reaching the longest periods with the strictest threshold. Levels that cannot be computed (fewer than 512 samples per segment, empty band) are reported as invalid rather than silently dropped.

    Lines, not bins¶

    Contiguous runs of significant bins are grouped into lines. A line's period, \(\gamma^2\) and amplitude ratio are those of its strongest bin (largest |Pxy|, not largest coherence — a wide coherent band's most coherent bin is often not the one carrying the power); its phase is the \(|P_{xy}|\)-weighted circular mean over all its bins (spread in phase_std). Lines are ranked by integrated cross-amplitude sum(|Pxy|).

    The phase criterion is applied to the top-ranked line only. Ranking matters: in the 30 Myr integration of 591986 the e–i search band holds tens of significant lines, one of which happens to sit at −166° and would satisfy a naive "is there any antiphase line" test. The amplitude-dominant line sits at +125° at every segmentation level, which is the correct answer.

    Phase criteria¶

    A criterion is (target_deg, tolerance_deg) per pair, preset per resonance type:

    resonance type pairs phase target search band
    lidov_kozai e-i 180° ± 30° periods > 10 kyr
    mmr sigma-a 90° ± 30° (for \(c > 0\), see above) full validity band
    secular sigma-e — periods > 10 kyr

    Phase sign convention: positive means the second series leads the first.

    Override per resonance type when creating the simulation; types you do not mention keep their presets:

    import resonances

sim = resonances.check(
    463,
    '4J-2S-1',
    integration_years=2_000,  # 100_000 years for a real verdict
    coherence_pairs={'mmr': ['sigma-a', 'sigma-e', 'sigma-i']},
    coherence_phase_targets={'mmr': {'sigma-a': (90.0, 20.0)}},
)

    Why phase alone is not enough¶

    A line's cross-phase is a weighted mean over a few adjacent bins, and those bins lie when several close lines blend into them. On 591986 the e–i line near 54 kyr is really a triplet (53.9 / 54.0 / 54.2 kyr). Truncating one 30 Myr integration to different lengths and running the same code gives:

    window decisive line phase band-passed r broadband r
    2 Myr 11.4 kyr −2.9° −0.25 −0.05
    3 Myr 11.0 kyr +29.5° −0.26 −0.02
    5 Myr 54.3 kyr +177.7° −0.15 −0.01
    7.5 Myr 27.2 kyr +134.0° −0.38 −0.00
    10–30 Myr 27–56 kyr +120…+128° −0.12…−0.18 ±0.00

    The phase says "antiphase" in exactly one window out of nine. The correlations say the same thing in all nine: e and i never actually trade. So the flag requires both.

    The e-i exchange flag¶

    Measured after the verdict, for Lidov-Kozai rows that are not non_resonant (with COHERENCE_SKIP_NON_RESONANT). It changes no status: it records whether the strongest coherent e–i line is an exchange, as zlk_coherent and zlk_check. The flag confirmed_ei and the check check_zlk_ei no longer come from here: they are the e–i coupling measured at the row's own decisive period, for every ZLK row (zlk_ei_coupling, Free elements: beyond the gate), because this line can sit on a harmonic or outside the ZLK band (3040, 4257 and 162474 pass here on lines at 10–21 kyr while their ZLK periods are 40–59 kyr).

    situation zlk_coherent zlk_check
    antiphase line and e/i anticorrelate True passed
    no antiphase line False failed
    antiphase line, but no exchange False weak_exchange
    baseline too short to measure empty short_baseline
    ZLK_COHERENCE_CHECK=False empty disabled
    no e-i pair, or no phase target for it empty no_target

    no_target appears when coherence_pairs or the phase targets of lidov_kozai were overridden without e-i; restoring coherence_pairs['lidov_kozai'] = ['e-i'] with its target enables the check.

    The 180° target is a prograde diagnostic. At constant \(H\) a retrograde orbit (\(\cos i < 0\)) has \(e\) and \(i\) varying in phase, and the anticorrelation test does not confirm that branch. The exchange also assumes \(i\) measured from the perturber's plane and \(H\) conserved (quadrupole approximation); this line uses the ecliptic \(i\) of an N-body run, so the comparison is approximate. The period-specific coupling (zlk_ei_coupling) reads \(i\) from the invariable plane and the tilt of a retrograde orbit. No status depends on either.

    short_baseline is the important distinction: a check that could not run must not be read as a negative result, so the reason is recorded and a researcher can filter on the column instead of guessing.

    Why it does not decide the status¶

    Earlier versions demoted a librating Lidov-Kozai angle on this check alone. The baseline is what took that away. The validity band tops out at T / (5 · n_seg), which is 25 kyr on a 500 kyr run at four segments, while the real Lidov-Kozai lines in the reference set sit at 27–58 kyr. On the short integrations this package now targets, the check would be judging on a line outside its own validity band — it happened to agree with the free-omega gate on all ten reference objects at 500 kyr, but by luck rather than by measurement.

    The status decision moved to the forced/free split, which has no such band: see free elements. The cross spectra, coherence.csv and the figures are unchanged; the line columns sit next to the status as independent evidence.

    The ZLK_MIN_ANTICORRELATION = 0.5 default is provisional. It sits between the only two objects measured so far — 591986 never exceeds 0.38 and is not in resonance, 162474 reaches 0.79 and is — but two objects do not calibrate a threshold. The raw r_band and r_broadband values are written to coherence.csv for every pair, so a larger sample can be collected and the threshold revisited from data.

    The correlation condition is deliberately ZLK-only: it encodes cos(180°) = −1, and would be meaningless against the MMR target of 90°, where quadrature puts the correlation at zero by construction. For MMR and secular resonances the cross spectrum is pure diagnostics.

    Forced and free eccentricity¶

    In the (k, h) = (e cos ω, e sin ω) plane the forced term is the offset of the cloud from the origin and the free term is the radius it traces:

    \[ e_{\rm forced} = \sqrt{\langle k\rangle^2 + \langle h\rangle^2}, \qquad e_{\rm free} = \sqrt{\operatorname{var} k + \operatorname{var} h}. \]

    Note the sum of the two variances. A free vector of radius R sweeping the circle uniformly puts \(R^2/2\) into each coordinate, so summing recovers R; averaging instead would under-report by a factor √2.

    Both go into summary.csv. On the 30 Myr integration of 591986 they come out as e_forced = 0.0392 and e_free = 0.0308, the latter matching the AstDyS tro.syn proper eccentricity e_p = 0.0308.

    Output¶

    summary.csv gains, per body and resonance: e_forced, e_free, zlk_coherent (True/False/empty), zlk_check, zlk_line_period, zlk_line_phase, zlk_line_gamma2, zlk_line_r_band.

    coherence.csv — one row per detected line, appended per run like summary.csv (written only when save_summary is on):

    column meaning
    body, resonance, pair what was analyzed
    n_seg, rank, decisive segmentation level, rank by cross-amplitude, whether this is the line the criterion used
    period, gamma2, gamma2_crit the line and the threshold it cleared
    phase_deg, phase_std cross-phase and its spread across the line's bins
    amp_ratio sqrt(Pyy/Pxx) — how much the second series moves per unit of the first
    r_band Pearson r of the two series band-passed around this line; filled for the strongest line only
    r_broadband Pearson r of the two raw series, unfiltered
    cross_amplitude, n_bins integrated \|Pxy\| and width of the line
    period_min, period_max validity band of that level
    phase_target, phase_tolerance criterion applied, if any
    n_levels_valid, n_levels_passed, n_levels_agreeing how many levels could be computed, how many satisfied the criterion, how many reproduce the decisive line

    amp_ratio is a useful independent check. Conservation of \(H = \sqrt{1-e^2}\cos i\) gives the signed derivative

    \[ \frac{{\rm d}i}{{\rm d}e} = -\frac{e\cos i}{(1 - e^2)\sin i}, \]

    so the amplitude ratio \(\lvert\delta i\rvert/\lvert\delta e\rvert\) (i in radians) should be the absolute value of the right-hand side (with \(i\) from the perturber's plane; for ecliptic elements this is approximate). The sign is negative only for a prograde orbit. amp_ratio is non-negative, an amplitude ratio, not the signed derivative. A line whose ratio is far from it is shared but not driven by the Lidov-Kozai mechanism.

    Plots¶

    Add to plots:

    sim = resonances.check(
    162474,
    'lidov-kozai',
    integration_years=20_000,  # 500 kyr or more for the Lidov-Kozai periods
    plots=['evolution', 'ecc_vector', 'cross_spectrum'],
    plot='all',
)
sim.run()
    • ecc_vector → {body}-ecc-vector.png (How to read the figures): the offset of the cloud is e_forced, its radius e_free. It explains why ω is bounded, not whether that is a resonance: genuine librators and forced-geometry cases both show an offset cloud, with e_free/e_forced below 1 for both (0.27–0.35 for 3040, 4257 and 162474, 0.79 for 591986).
    • cross_spectrum → {body}-{resonance}-coherence-{pair}.png per pair: coherence, cross-phase and the two series band-passed at the decisive line (How to read the figures). The yellow bands there mark agreement across levels at one of the strongest lines; agreement alone is reported but is not a gate condition, since for e-i it mostly measures the conservation of H = √(1−e²)·cos i: the share of the band where all levels agree tracks how well i is predicted from e through H (3040 78 %, 4257 44 %, 88899 24 %, 10563 0 %; R² = 0.86, 0.64, 0.06, 0).

    Both plotters are usable directly:

    body = sim.bodies[0]
resonance = body.lidov_kozai_resonances[0]  # key 'LK'
resonances.EccentricityVectorPlotter.from_body(body, resonance, sim).plot().save('kh.png')
resonances.CrossSpectrumPlotter.from_body(body, resonance, 'e-i').plot().save('coh.png')

    Using the primitives directly¶

    import numpy as np  # noqa: E402

times_yrs = np.asarray(sim.times) / (2 * np.pi)  # a uniform grid, in years
e, inc = body.ecc, body.inc  # a, e and i are not unwrapped; angles must be (np.unwrap)

result = resonances.cross_spectrum(times_yrs, e, inc, n_seg=4, alpha=0.01, period_min=10_000)
if result.valid and result.top_line:
    line = result.top_line
    print(line.period, line.gamma2, line.phase_deg, line.amp_ratio)
    print(resonances.phase_matches(line.phase_deg, 180.0, 30.0))

    Angles must be np.unwraped before being passed in; a, e and i must not be. The grid must be uniform — a non-uniform one returns valid=False with a reason rather than a wrong number.
