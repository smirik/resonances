    Lidov-Kozai resonances¶

    In the classical prograde quadrupole problem the Lidov-Kozai (ZLK) resonance is a libration of the argument of perihelion \(\omega\) about 90° or 270°, with \(e\) and \(i\) varying in antiphase at constant \(\sqrt{1-e^2}\cos i\). Inside a mean-motion resonance the center can shift, so the classifier tests that \(\omega\) and its free counterpart are confined (free-omega gate), not that the center is at 90° or 270°; the e–i phase is a flag (Coherence). The package checks it as a resonance with the angle \(\omega\): resonances.LidovKozaiResonance(), key LK. A run may combine it with MMRs and secular resonances.

    Lidov's integrals¶

    In the quadrupole approximation of the doubly averaged, circular restricted three-body problem (an inner body, a distant perturber), \(a\) is constant and the motion has two more integrals (Lidov 1962, eqs. 58–60; Kozai 1962, eqs. 26–28):

    \[ c_1 = (1 - e^2)\cos^2 i, \qquad c_2 = e^2\left(\tfrac{2}{5} - \sin^2 i \,\sin^2\omega\right). \]

    Here \(i\) is the inclination to the perturber's orbital plane and \(\omega\) is measured from the node on that plane; \(c_1\) is then the squared normalized component of the angular momentum normal to the perturber's orbit. In that limit \(\omega\) librates about 90° or 270° when \(c_1 < 3/5\) and \(c_2 < 0\), and circulates otherwise; for an initially circular orbit this needs \(\arccos\sqrt{3/5} \approx 39.2° < i < 140.8°\) (not a classifier threshold). LidovKozaiParameters.evaluate rotates nothing: it uses the angles it is given. The package reads them from the invariable plane (evaluate_invariable: the angular momentum and the perihelion rotated exactly, no angle subtracted), the plane of the planets' total angular momentum, as an approximate Solar-system stand-in for the perturber's plane. The summary columns c1, c2, h_quad are these: osculating, at the epoch, invariable plane. From the ecliptic \(c_1\) moves by up to 0.02 and the sign of \(c_2\) can flip (1373 Cincinnati: \(+0.0014\) ecliptic, \(-0.0011\) invariable). The quadrupole energy, without its constant factor, is

    \[ h_\text{quad} = (2 + 3e^2)(3\cos^2 i - 1) + 15 e^2 \sin^2 i \cos 2\omega = 6c_1 - 2 + 30 c_2 . \]
    import numpy as np

import resonances
from resonances import LidovKozaiParameters

# osculating ecliptic e, i, Omega, omega of three main-belt asteroids (the benchmark's epoch)
samples = {
    '1373 Cincinnati': (0.3152, 38.953, -62.561, 98.882),
    '3040 Kozai': (0.2003, 46.647, 143.473, 290.213),
    '15527': (0.1529, 21.259, 173.771, 168.872),
}
for name, (e, inc_deg, node_deg, omega_deg) in samples.items():
    # i and omega read from the invariable plane (an exact rotation), as the summary columns c1, c2, h_quad
    c1, c2, h_quad = LidovKozaiParameters.evaluate_invariable(e, *np.radians([inc_deg, node_deg, omega_deg]))
    print(f'{name:16s} c1 = {c1:.3f}  c2 = {c2:+.4f}  h_quad = {h_quad:.3f}')

    1373 Cincinnati  c1 = 0.521  c2 = -0.0011  h_quad = 1.091
3040 Kozai       c1 = 0.474  c2 = -0.0021  h_quad = 0.778
15527            c1 = 0.855  c2 = +0.0092  h_quad = 3.404

    These numbers are diagnostics, not the verdict. The criterion is exact only for \(a/a_\text{pert} \to 0\); a main-belt asteroid has \(a/a_J \approx 0.5\) and several perturbers, and a value this close to zero (1373's \(c_2 = -0.0011\); \(+0.0014\) from the ecliptic) says nothing on its own. The sign of \(h_\text{quad}\) is not a criterion either: \(e = 0.1\), \(i = 70°\), \(\omega = 0\) gives \(h_\text{quad} < 0\) while \(\omega\) circulates (\(c_2 > 0\)). The osculating elements are used here, at one instant.

    Checking a body¶

    sim = resonances.Simulation(
    name='lk-demo',
    date='2026-01-01',
    integration_years=5_000,  # 500_000 years for a real verdict: the omega cycles last 10-100 kyr
    integrator='SABA(10,6,4)',
    dt=0.5,  # REBOUND units: 0.5 / (2 pi) yr = 29 days
    output_step=10.0,  # years
    plot_always=['lidov_kozai'],  # draw the omega figure whatever the status
)
sim.create_solar_system()
for number in (1373, 3040, 15527):
    sim.add_body(number, resonances.LidovKozaiResonance(), name=str(number))
sim.run()

for body in sim.bodies:
    verdict = body.verdict('LK')
    print(body.name, verdict.status, verdict.angle_status, verdict.reason, verdict.libration_origin)

    The cycles of \(\omega\) last tens of thousands of years, so a verdict needs a long record: 500 000 years with a saved point every 10 years is a good default. SABA(10,6,4) with dt = 0.5 (29 days) is safe for perihelion distances \(q \gtrsim 1.3\) au; closer to the planets use ias15 (dry-run). The short run above gives uncertain / insufficient_time; over 500 000 years 3040 Kozai is resonant with certain confidence (Gallery).

    The angle is a series like any other:

    body = sim.bodies[1]  # 3040 Kozai
t = sim.times / (2 * np.pi)  # years
omega = body.angle(resonances.LidovKozaiResonance())  # radians in [0, 2 pi)
print(f'{len(t)} points, omega from {np.degrees(omega.min()):.0f} to {np.degrees(omega.max()):.0f} deg')

    How the status is decided¶

    \(\omega\), \(i\) and \(\Omega\) are measured from the invariable plane (ZLK_ANGLE_FRAME = invariable, the default): the orbit's angular momentum and perihelion are rotated exactly, as for Lidov's integrals. The free elements, the diagnostics below and the figure read the same frame. From the ecliptic, the node of an orbit tilted from the invariable plane by about the plane's own 1.58° or less librates about the plane's node instead of turning, and \(\omega = \varpi - \Omega\) with it: 555527 (\(i_\text{inv} \approx 1.6°\)) looked bounded from the ecliptic and circulates from the invariable plane. Where the two frames' \(\omega\) part by a whole turn or more, the row carries the rule zlk_invariable_frame (column zlk_frame_shift_turns). The integration stores the ecliptic \(\omega\); the analysis writes the classified angle back, so the data files hold it too.

    The angle \(\omega\) goes through the same geometry as every other angle (turning points, cycles, episodes; What the angle does). A libration of the osculating \(\omega\) is not enough: the forced eccentricity, driven by the planets, can hold the osculating perihelion while the body's own (free) perihelion circulates. The free-ω gate splits \(e\) and \(i\) into forced and free parts and asks whether the free \(\omega\) is confined (Free elements, Secular and Lidov-Kozai).

    On a librating \(\omega\) the gate's outcome sets the status: confirmed keeps resonant, kinematic gives non_resonant / kinematic_libration, no verdict gives uncertain / gate_unavailable; the full rule, also for episodes and short records: Secular and Lidov-Kozai. Gallery shows the Jupiter Trojan 591986, whose osculating \(\omega\) librates while its free \(\omega\) winds.

    Four measurements sit next to the status and change none of it (Free elements):

    • e–i coupling at the row's decisive period (zlk_ei_coupling, every row whatever its status): zlk when \(e\) and the inclination to the invariable plane trade in antiphase at the amplitude of constant \(\sqrt{1-e^2}\cos i\), forced_beat when they move in phase at the amplitude of constant \(\sqrt{1-e^2}(1-\cos i)\) (a forced eccentricity beating against the free one), none, or not_measured. Coupling is not libration: 15527 circulates and still trades \(e\) for \(i\) at its 2.05-kyr \(\cos 2\omega\) harmonic. confirmed_ei (and check_zlk_ei) is zlk_ei_coupling == zlk.
    • Attribution of a kinematic libration (zlk_forcing_*): the forced mode that holds \(\varpi\) (591986: g5 with the modes merged into it), when the free circle stays off the origin, the predicted beat is the libration period and the node follows the mode (an episodic row demoted inside its episodes: scope episodes, the node free to turn); otherwise "forcing not attributed". The figure states it as the cause and draws panel (a′).
    • Node lock (zlk_node_lock, a flag): the node's rate one line with a forced mode's and \(\Omega - \varpi_j\) librating through at least one full cycle (591986 over 4 Myr: g5; over 500 kyr not resolved).
    • The slow line of the center (zlk_centre_*): its period and half-amplitude, and its origin (591986: the g6−g5 beat of the forced vector, 54 kyr, ±22°).
    • Co-orbitals of Mercury to Mars have no gate verdict (zlk_gate_detail = coorbital_unfitted_forcing): their forced term is the planet's own vector, which the split does not fit; Jupiter's Trojans are covered.
