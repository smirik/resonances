    Quick start¶

    Three checks: a mean-motion resonance, a secular resonance and a Lidov-Kozai resonance. Each integrates the orbit of an asteroid with the planets, builds the resonant angle and gives it a verdict (How the status is determined).

    A mean-motion resonance¶

    (463) Lola is in the three-body resonance 4J-2S-1, with the angle \(\sigma = 4\lambda_J - 2\lambda_S - \lambda - \varpi\) (Resonance matrices explain the notation).

    import resonances

# (463) Lola in the three-body resonance 4J-2S-1 (4 lambda_J - 2 lambda_S - lambda + ...)
sim = resonances.check(463, '4J-2S-1', integration_years=2_000)  # a real verdict: integration_years=100_000, output_step=1.0
sim.run()

body = sim.bodies[0]
for key, verdict in body.librations.items():  # key: the full angle, 4J-2S-1+0+0-1
    print(key, verdict.status, verdict.angle_status, verdict.confidence, verdict.reason, verdict.action)
print(sim.config.save_path)  # summary.csv, the series and the figures are written here

    check downloads the elements from NASA Horizons at today's date, integrates, analyses the angle and saves the results. The code above runs only 2 000 years, so the verdict is

    4J-2S-1+0+0-1 uncertain insufficient_time  baseline_needed extend

    The libration period of Lola is about 10 000 years: 2 000 years hold no full cycle, the status is uncertain, the reason baseline_needed, and the suggested action extend (verdict.extend_to_years says how long to integrate). With integration_years=100_000, output_step=1.0 the verdict is (from the Gallery run: 100 kyr, step 1 yr, SABA(10,6,4); not re-run by the script above)

    status angle_status reason confidence action
    resonant libration dynamic_libration high none

    The output step matters: without output_step the default step of a 100 kyr run is about 15.9 yr, longer than half of Lola's 3.7-yr orbit, so the record folds short periods: the flag short_period_aliased says so (Dry run).

    The fields:

    • status: the answer, one of resonant, transient, near_separatrix, non_resonant, uncertain, invalid;
    • angle_status: what the angle does over the whole record (libration, episodic, circulation, ...);
    • reason and libration_origin: why (dynamic_libration: the angle librates and the semi-major axis follows it as the pendulum of this resonance requires);
    • confidence: how well the evidence supports the status (certain, high, medium, low);
    • action: what to do next (none, spot_check, review, extend, resample).

    The overview defines every value. All of them are also in summary.csv:

    summary = sim.data_manager.get_simulation_summary(sim.bodies)
print(summary[['name', 'resonance', 'status', 'angle_status', 'confidence', 'reason', 'action']])

    The figure¶

    For every row selected by the plot mode (by default review: everything except a confident non_resonant angle that nothing bounds, such as a plain circulation; doubtful negatives are kept for review) a one-page figure is saved in <save_path>/combined/<confidence>/<status>/: the angle, the semi-major axis and the pendulum's energy in time on the left, and the verdict with every number behind it on the right.     A secular resonance¶

    (1222) Tina is in the linear secular resonance \(\nu_6\), g-g6: the angle is \(\varpi - (g_6 t + \phi_6)\), the body's longitude of perihelion against the phase of the planetary secular mode \(g_6\) (by default the body's forced terms at the other planetary frequencies are removed first; Secular and Lidov-Kozai).

    # (1222) Tina in the linear secular resonance nu6 (angle varpi - varpi of Saturn's mode g6)
sim = resonances.check(1222, 'g-g6', integration_years=5_000)  # 2-10 Myr for a real verdict
sim.run()
print(sim.bodies[0].statuses)  # {'g-g6': 'uncertain'} on this short run

    Secular angles librate with periods of hundreds of thousands of years, so a real check needs 2–10 Myr (integration_years=2_000_000 or more); over 10 Myr Tina is resonant with certain confidence (Gallery). See Secular resonances.

    A Lidov-Kozai resonance¶

    The Lidov-Kozai angle is the argument of perihelion \(\omega\). (3040) Kozai librates about 270°:

    # (3040) Kozai: the Lidov-Kozai angle is the argument of perihelion omega
sim = resonances.check(3040, resonances.LidovKozaiResonance(), integration_years=5_000)  # 500 kyr for a real verdict
sim.run()
print(sim.bodies[0].statuses)  # {'LK': ...}

    Over 500 000 years the verdict is resonant with certain confidence (Gallery). See Lidov-Kozai resonances.
