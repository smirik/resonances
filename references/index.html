    References¶

    The physics pages cite these works. Page and equation numbers refer to the printed pages.

    Resonant dynamics

    • Murray, C. D., & Dermott, S. F. (1999). Solar System Dynamics. Cambridge University Press. Used: co-orbital frequencies (eq. 3.151, p. 94; eqs. 3.178–3.179, p. 105), the disturbing function (eq. 6.13, p. 227), Lagrange's equation for \(a\) (eq. 6.145, p. 251), secular vectors (eqs. 7.18–7.19, p. 277), the resonant angle and its rate (eq. 8.18, p. 329; eq. 8.22, p. 331), the pendulum model, its period and width (eqs. 8.45–8.58, pp. 335–338).
    • Morbidelli, A. (2002). Modern Celestial Mechanics: Aspects of Solar System Dynamics. Taylor & Francis. Used: the pendulum frequency \(\sqrt{\beta c}\) of a resonant normal form (Eqs. 4.3–4.4, pp. 72–74), single-resonance normal forms and three-body terms at second order (§9.3, pp. 221–223), multiplets and modulated resonances (§9.4, pp. 224–226).
    • Gallardo, T. (2006). Atlas of the mean motion resonances in the Solar System. Icarus, 184, 29–38. doi:10.1016/j.icarus.2006.04.001. Used: the averaged disturbing function at fixed \(\sigma\) (eqs. 1–6, p. 30) and the stable centers (§3).
    • Nesvorný, D., & Morbidelli, A. (1998). Three-body mean motion resonances and the chaotic structure of the asteroid belt. Astronomical Journal, 116, 3029–3037. doi:10.1086/300632. Used: three-body angles (eqs. 1–2, p. 3029), filtering before decimation (§2, p. 3030), membership judged by the correlation of the filtered \(a\) with the angle (§2, pp. 3030–3031), the strength scale of three-body resonances (§3, pp. 3031–3032).
    • Gallardo, T., Coito, L., & Badano, L. (2016). Planetary and satellite three body mean motion resonances. Icarus, 274, 83–98. doi:10.1016/j.icarus.2016.03.018. Used: two critical angles that both librate while only one moves \(a\) (p. 89).
    • Nesvorný, D., & Morbidelli, A. (1998). An analytic model of three-body mean motion resonances. Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy, 71, 243–271. doi:10.1023/A:1008347020890.
    • Wisdom, J. (1985). A perturbative treatment of motion near the 3/1 commensurability. Icarus, 63, 272–289. doi:10.1016/0019-1035(85)90011-9. Used: the action of the pendulum (§III, p. 277) and adiabatic changes of the well (pp. 274, 279).
    • Henrard, J., & Lemaître, A. (1983). A second fundamental model for resonance. Celestial Mechanics, 30, 197–218. doi:10.1007/BF01234306.
    • Lemaître, A. (1984). High-order resonances in the restricted three-body problem. Celestial Mechanics, 32, 109–126. doi:10.1007/BF01231119.

    • Henrard, J. (1982). Capture into resonance: an extension of the use of adiabatic invariants. Celestial Mechanics, 27, 3–22.

    • Li, M., Huang, Y., & Gong, S. (2019). Survey of asteroids in retrograde mean motion resonances with planets. Astronomy & Astrophysics, 630, A60. doi:10.1051/0004-6361/201936117. Used: the angle of a retrograde resonance.

    Secular resonances: libration periods

    • Morbidelli, A., & Henrard, J. (1991). Secular resonances in the asteroid belt: theoretical perturbation approach and the problem of their location. Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy, 51, 131–167. doi:10.1007/BF00048606. Used: the local resonant Hamiltonian (Eqs. 35–39, pp. 147–149).
    • Milani, A., & Knežević, Z. (1992). Asteroid proper elements and secular resonances. Icarus, 98, 211–232. doi:10.1016/0019-1035(92)90091-K. Used: \(z_1\) libration periods of (339) Dorothea and (221) Eos (p. 227).
    • Vokrouhlický, D., Brož, M., Bottke, W. F., Nesvorný, D., & Morbidelli, A. (2006). The peculiar case of the Agnia asteroid family. Icarus, 183, 349–361. doi:10.1016/j.icarus.2006.03.002. Used: the \(z_1\) cycle of (3701) Purkyne (Fig. 4).
    • Carruba, V., Aljbaae, S., & Winter, O. C. (2016). On the Erigone family and the \(z_2\) secular resonance. Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 455, 2279–2288. doi:10.1093/mnras/stv2430. Used: the \(z_2\) cycle of (8089) Yukar (Fig. 3, pp. 2281–2282) and its portrait in Charlier variables (Fig. 3B), the model of panel (k).

    Lidov-Kozai

    • Lidov, M. L. (1962). The evolution of orbits of artificial satellites of planets under the action of gravitational perturbations of external bodies. Planetary and Space Science, 9, 719–759. doi:10.1016/0032-0633(62)90129-0. Used: the two integrals \(c_1, c_2\) (eqs. 58–60, p. 741).
    • Kozai, Y. (1962). Secular perturbations of asteroids with high inclination and eccentricity. Astronomical Journal, 67, 591–598. doi:10.1086/108790. Used: the quadrupole energy integral (eqs. 24, 26–28, p. 594).
    • Shevchenko, I. I. (2017). The Lidov-Kozai Effect — Applications in Exoplanet Research and Dynamical Astronomy. Astrophysics and Space Science Library 441, Springer. doi:10.1007/978-3-319-43522-0. Used: Lidov's integrals \(c_1, c_2\) and the libration criterion (eqs. 3.35–3.36, pp. 35–36; eq. 3.43, p. 42), the quadrupole Hamiltonian in the Kinoshita–Nakai form (eq. 3.41, p. 41).

    Signal processing and statistics

    • Knežević, Z., & Milani, A. (2000). Synthetic proper elements for outer main belt asteroids. Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy, 78, 17–46 (reprinted in Dvorak, R. & Henrard, J., eds., New Developments in the Dynamics of Planetary Systems, Springer, 2001). Used: aliasing of a sampled angle and filtering before decimation (§2.2, pp. 22–23).
    • Wilson, E. B. (1927). Probable inference, the law of succession, and statistical inference. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 22, 209–212. doi:10.1080/01621459.1927.10502953.
