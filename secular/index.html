    Secular resonances¶

    A secular resonance is a commensurability between the slow precession of the body's perihelion (g) or node (s) and the fundamental frequencies of the planets (g1…g8, s1…s8, Mercury…Neptune). How the status of a secular angle is decided: Secular and Lidov-Kozai.

    Creating resonances¶

    import resonances

nu6 = resonances.create_secular_resonance('nu6')  # g-g6
nu14 = resonances.create_secular_resonance('nu14')  # s-s4
z1 = resonances.create_secular_resonance('g-g6+s-s6')
z2 = resonances.create_secular_resonance('2(g-g6)+(s-s6)')
other = resonances.create_secular_resonance('-g+s+g5-s7')

    Aliases: nu1…nu8 are g-g1…g-g8, nu11…nu18 are s-s1…s-s8 (no nu15: s5 is identically zero), z1…z4, 2nu6-nu5, 3nu6-2nu5. A formula needs at least one term of the body (g or s); planet indices run from 1 to 8. A string is recognized as secular by detect_resonance_type if it is an alias (any case) or its only letters are a lowercase g and s. Capital letters are MMR notation, so 2S-1 and 4J-2S-1 are MMRs with Saturn.

    How the angle is built¶

    With secular_angle_mode='proper' (the default), the angle that is classified, plotted and saved compares the body's perihelion and node with the planets' mode longitudes \(g_j t + \phi_j\) (the frequencies PLANETARY_FREQUENCIES and the J2000 phases PLANETARY_PHASES of resonances/data/const.py, shifted to the simulation date), with the body's node measured from the invariable plane (INVARIABLE_PLANE). The formulas and the fitted basis: The secular angle. This section explains why.

    Both matter. Measured from the ecliptic, the node of every planet is dominated by the constant 1.58° tilt of the invariable plane and does not circulate at all (Jupiter's and Saturn's mean rate is 0, not s6); the osculating perihelia of Venus, Earth, Mars, Uranus and Neptune do not turn at their own mode either (Uranus' turns at ~5.8″/yr, not g7). Only Mercury's, Jupiter's and Saturn's perihelia follow their mode. So an angle built from the planets' osculating elements is wrong for every term but g1, g5, g6: built so, the z1 angle g+s-g6-s6 of (847) Agnia turns at −26″/yr, exactly −s6, the rate of the planets' artificial node. The same holds for a body of low free inclination, whose ecliptic node librates around the invariable node instead of turning at s.

    Reduced angle (secular_remove_forced=True, the default). The body's e and i vectors carry forced terms at every planetary fundamental. Those at frequencies that are not in the angle (g5 for z1 or ν6, s7 for z2, …) only shake it, and when the free e or i is not much larger than the forced one they decide whether the angle turns at all: (363) Padua's z1 angle from the osculating elements circulates for 6 of 10 Myr while the resonance holds. The literature removes them by low-pass filtering (Carruba et al. 2016 filter the z2 angle at 700 kyr "to eliminate all short-period perturbations such as the g5 frequency"); here they are subtracted by least squares at the known frequencies (proper_angle.reduced_vectors). The terms at the angle's own frequencies (g6, s6 for z1; g6 for ν6) are the resonance and stay — one rule for linear and nonlinear resonances. Removing all of them would give the free-phase angle of Vokrouhlický et al. (2006, Agnia), which for a linear resonance removes the resonance itself.

    The angle from the osculating elements (same mode longitudes, nothing removed) is saved as <resonance>_angle_osculating; secular_angle_mode='osculating' classifies the integration's angle instead. The analysis of the angle (cutoff, center scale, turning points: a libration needs LIBRATION_MIN_TURNING_POINTS = 3 turning points, one full cycle) is described in Secular and Lidov-Kozai.

    Free divisor (summary.csv free_divisor, ″/yr; a diagnostic, no status depends on it): \(p g + q s + \sum_j(p_j g_j + q_j s_j)\) with the body's own \(g\) and \(s\) — the rates of its free vectors, every fundamental removed (proper_angle.free_rates). Near 0 the free vector itself keeps pace with the planets' modes (16712 and 48717 in ν6, the z1 and z2 librators). A libration whose period is \(P = 1\,296\,000/|D|\) yr, with the free divisor \(D\) in ″/yr (one turn = 1 296 000″), is one around the forced equilibrium: the angle's own forced term is larger than the free amplitude and the free vector turns at its own rate — Tina in ν6 (2.18″/yr, 592 kyr), the anti-aligned ν6 libration of Carruba & Morbidelli (2011). The two cannot be told apart by this number alone, so it changes nothing.

    Planetary frequencies and phases¶

    g5…g8, s6…s8 are the AstDyS values (Knežević & Milani 2000). g1…g4, s1…s4 and all the phases are measured on the package's own model (Sun, eight planets, Pluto, Newtonian, Earth = Earth–Moon barycenter) by a frequency analysis of a 10-Myr integration. Against Laskar (1990, Icarus 88, 266, Tables II–III) the outer planets agree to 0.01–0.02″/yr and 1–2° in phase, g3, g4, s3, s4 to 0.01–0.06″/yr; g1 is lower by 0.38″/yr, g2 by 0.12, s1 higher by 0.11 — the model has no general relativity. The inner frequencies drift by ~0.01″/yr over 10 Myr, and their phases are uncertain by tens of degrees. s2 is split into several lines in this model and is the least certain of all.

    Measured phases (secular_planet_phases='measured', needs save_planets='all' (or True)). Over tens of Myr the linear law drifts away from the integrated planets: in a 20 Myr run of the package's model the phase of s4 is off by 80° after 17 Myr, s3 by 23°, g3 and s2 by 20°, and even s6 by 16° (the AstDyS s6 is 0.003″/yr off the model's). resonances/secular/planet_modes.py fits the phase of mode j in planet j's own vector (e·exp(iϖ) for g_j, sin(i/2)·exp(iΩ) for s_j) by least squares on all eight modes of that kind, in windows of secular_phase_window (5 Myr: g3 and g4 are 0.54″/yr apart), a tenth of a window apart, and builds the longitude as g_j t + φ_j(t). Within half a window of either end the phase goes on along its slope. A baseline shorter than the window keeps the linear law: a shorter fit cannot separate g3 from g4 (over 200 kyr it puts g3 51° off).

    Mode phases¶

    How far the linear phases stray (resonances/data/mode_phase_uncertainty.json). For every mode, range_j(T) is the largest max − min of φ_j(t) over a window of length T, from the same 5 Myr fits, in three runs of the model (SABA dt 0.5 and 0.25 over 50 Myr, WHFast 3 d over 20 Myr), the maximum over the runs. Up to 20 Myr the windows lie anywhere inside the first 20 Myr from the epoch, the span the three runs agree on; a longer T starts at the epoch (both SABA runs show a jump of the inner modes 22–25 Myr ahead, where g3 moves by 125° in 2.5 Myr). At T = 10 Myr: g1 18°, g2 2.7°, g3 24°, g4 30°, s1 17°, s2 87°, s3 23°, s4 81°; every outer mode below 10° (s6 9.5°, g5 1.4°, g6 0.9°). The fit smooths φ_j over 5 Myr, so below that length range_j is the phase's drift, not its faster wobble. Under the linear law a secular libration must swing more than Σ|k_j| range_j(T) over the angle's modes, or it is insufficient_time / phase_unresolved (the rule). The table was measured with a research script on local runs; neither is part of the package.

    Which resonances to check¶

    SECULAR_FORMULAS is the catalog: the linear ν_j, ν_1j of all planets, the classical degree-4 set, z1…z4 and the g-type terms (75 distinct angles). For a complete search around one body use resonances.secular.combinations.near_resonant(g, s, max_order=6, thresholds={2: 5.0, 4: 2.0, 6: 1.0}, modes=...) (g, s and the windows in ″/yr; pass modes by name). For \(\sigma = p\varpi + q\Omega + \sum_j(p_j\varpi_j + q_j\Omega_j)\) it builds every angle allowed by the d'Alembert rules, \(p + q + \sum_j(p_j + q_j) = 0\) and \(q + \sum_j q_j\) even (the sum of the node coefficients, not the number of node terms: 2s − s4 − s6 is allowed), up to the order \(|p| + |q| + \sum_j(|p_j| + |q_j|)\), and keeps those whose divisor \(p g + q s + \sum_j(p_j g_j + q_j s_j)\) is inside the window for its order. It returns SecularCombination records (formula, order, divisor, key); resonances.create_secular_resonance(formula) turns a chosen formula into a resonance. Order 6 with all fifteen planetary modes gives ~900 angles within 1″/yr of a main-belt body — the window and the modes have to be chosen physically.

    The catalog is wider than what the literature reports. Librations are shown for ν5, ν6, ν16, ν4 (one Hungaria), z1, z2, g+g5−2g6, g+2g5−3g6, 2g−3g5+g6, g−g5+s−s7, 2g−g5−g6+s−s6 and, among the Hungarias, g−s−g5+s6; s−g5+g8−s6, s+g5−g8−s3, 2s−s4−s6 and 2g−g5−g6 are located there but shown only as temporary interactions (Warner et al. 2009; Milani et al. 2010; Knežević 2022). Nothing is reported for combinations with g1, g2, s1, s2 or mixed degree-4 terms of the inner planets with Uranus or Neptune; their divisors in this model are also off by 0.1–0.3″/yr (no general relativity). A degree-4 resonance (half-width ~0.3″/yr) librates in ~9 Myr, a degree-6 one (~0.1″/yr) in ~26 Myr. To plan the baseline, use the rule \(T_{\rm req}\) of Confidence (one and a half periods plus the two filter edges).

    SecularResonanceFinder evaluates the catalog's divisors on AstDyS synthetic proper elements (resonances secular-candidates on the command line).

    Example: (1020) Arcadia in z1¶

    The benchmark example of a z1 librator (SABA(10,6,4), dt = 0.5, 10 Myr from 2026-09-29, a 100-yr output step; the benchmark keeps every fifth point, a 500-yr step, which the Gallery verdict reads):

    sim = resonances.Simulation(
    date='2026-09-29',
    integrator='SABA(10,6,4)',
    dt=0.5,
    integration_years=20_000,  # the benchmark run: integration_years=10_000_000, Nout=100001
    Nout=201,
)
sim.create_solar_system()
sim.add_body(1020, [resonances.create_secular_resonance('z1')], name='Arcadia')
sim.run()
print(sim.bodies[0].statuses)  # {'g-g6+s-s6': 'resonant'} over 10 Myr in the benchmark; 20 kyr is too short

    (847) Agnia, the classical z1 family, circulates slowly in the same benchmark (Gallery): its free vector keeps near pace with the planets' modes without turning back.
